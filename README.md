# Thrive Hackathon 2026 Kenyatta University
## Project: Circular Waste Hub

> *Developing a sustainable circular economy by matching local waste generators with regional recyclers, agricultural producers, and upcycling artisans.*

---

## Executive Overview

[**Elevator pitch**](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1shMFkwEGbdpZ18kfwikpboHs-52Rnptu/edit?usp=drive_link&ouid=108946103952858474687&rtpof=true&sd=true)

**Circular Waste Hub** is a digital resource-exchange platform created for the **THRIVE Hackathon**. It directly addresses urban, commercial, and agricultural waste challenges by establishing a decentralized waste-to-resource exchange system.

Rather than sending recyclable and organic byproducts directly to municipal landfills, **Circular Waste Hub** operates as a dynamic matching ecosystem. It enables commercial businesses, market vendors, and institutions to offload waste efficiently while supplying local recyclers, livestock farmers, and creative builders with low-cost raw materials.

---

## Problem Statement & Market Opportunity

Traditional linear waste management ("Take-Make-Dispose") creates severe economic inefficiencies and environmental degradation:

* **High Disposal Expenses:** Commercial establishments, grocery stores, and agricultural vendors face high logistics costs and disposal fees to clear unsellable inventory and production byproducts.
* **Material Scarcity & High Input Costs:** Local livestock farmers, organic fertilizer producers, and small-scale recycling enterprises struggle to secure reliable, low-cost raw materials.
* **Environmental Impact:** Organic waste rotting in municipal landfills undergoes anaerobic decomposition, releasing methane gases and leaching contaminants into surrounding soil and water tables.

---

## The Circular Solution & Matching System

**Circular Waste Hub** replaces linear waste flows with a closed-loop circular exchange. The system serves as an intermediary listing and matching hub that categorizes materials by classification, volume, perishability, geographic proximity, and pickup schedules.

```
+-----------------------------------+
|          WASTE GENERATOR          |
| (Supermarkets, Market Vendors,    |
|  Restaurants, Institutions)       |

```

## Practical Application Examples

### 1. Organic Waste to Livestock Feed
* **Scenario:** A market vendor accumulates unsellable or overripe produce (e.g., surplus or rotting tomatoes).
* **Exchange:** The vendor posts the batch on Circular Waste Hub. The platform matches the listing with a nearby pig farmer who collects the produce to supplement feed costs.

### 2. Commercial Plastics to Local Upcycling
* **Scenario:** A supermarket generates substantial volumes of clean polyethylene plastic bottles and film packaging.
* **Exchange:** The waste is listed and routed to a regional recycling plant or a local artisan who upcycles plastics into artisan goods, construction blocks, or household crafts.

### 3. Food Byproducts to Agricultural Compost
* **Scenario:** Restaurants and coffee shops generate significant daily volumes of organic food scraps and spent coffee grounds.
* **Exchange:** Local urban farming cooperatives and composting initiatives receive real-time notifications to arrange routine pickups for soil enrichment.

---

## Core System Functionalities

* **Resource Classification Taxonomy:** Categorizes waste into structured streams (e.g., Organic/Perishable, Polymer Grades 1–7, Scrap Fiber, Wood Byproduct).
* **Proximity-Based Matching:** Uses geographic coordinates to match generators with nearby collectors, minimizing transportation overhead and vehicle emissions.
* **Scheduling & Handover Protocol:** Provides built-in scheduling tools to define pickup windows, handling specifications, and collection status.
* **Impact & Diverted Mass Tracking:** Calculates total waste weight (kg/tons) diverted from landfills to help organizations track sustainability metrics.

---

## Target Ecosystem Stakeholders

* **Generators:** Supermarkets, agricultural markets, food processors, educational institutions, and commercial venues.
* **Consumers:** Livestock farmers, composting operations, industrial recyclers, scrap processors, and craft artisans.
* **Community & Environment:** Municipalities benefit from lowered landfill pressure, reduced municipal waste management expenditure, and reduced informal dumping.
